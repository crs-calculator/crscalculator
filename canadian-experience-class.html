Canadian Experience Class (CEC): Requirements, Explained

Last updated: November 18, 2026. Sources: IRCC Canadian Experience Class page and Express Entry eligibility comparison page, read November 2026.

The Canadian Experience Class (CEC) is the Express Entry program for people with skilled Canadian work experience. Unlike the Federal Skilled Worker Program, there is no points grid, no education requirement, and no proof of funds. If you have 1 year of skilled work in Canada in the last 3 years and meet the language minimum for your job's Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities (TEER) level, you can qualify. This guide covers the work rules, hour counting, language table, and the mistakes that most often cause refusals.

Qualifying for CEC only gets you into the pool. Your CRS score then decides whether you receive an invitation, so check your ranking with the CRS Calculator once you know you qualify.

Who CEC is for

CEC is for skilled workers who have already worked in Canada and want to become permanent residents. Your work must be in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation, and you must plan to live outside Quebec. Quebec selects its own skilled workers, so CEC applicants must intend to live in another province or territory.

Work experience rules and hour counting

You need 1 year of skilled Canadian work experience in the last 3 years. That means 1,560 hours. Full-time work is up to 30 hours a week for 12 months. Work above 30 hours a week does not shorten the 12 months. Your work can be full-time, part-time, or a combination of both, as long as the hours add up and the work is in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation.

Work patternTime to reach 1,560 hours
30 hours a week (full-time)12 months
15 hours a week (part-time)24 months
A combination of jobsHours add up across jobs, within the last 3 years

Only work in the last 3 years counts. Older Canadian work experience does not help you qualify for CEC, although it may still earn CRS points under the Labour Market Integration factor.

What does not count

  • Work while studying full-time: work gained while you were a full-time student does not count, including co-op work terms.
  • Self-employment: self-employed work does not count, with an exception for some physicians.
  • Work below TEER 3: work in TEER 4 or 5 occupations does not count toward CEC.
  • Work in Quebec: work gained in Quebec counts toward the work experience itself, but you must plan to live outside Quebec to qualify.

Language requirements

Your minimum language level depends on the TEER level of your job. The level applies to all four abilities: listening, reading, writing and speaking.

Job TEER levelMinimum CLB or NCLC, all four abilities
TEER 0 or 1CLB 7
TEER 2 or 3CLB 5

Language test results must be less than 2 years old. Use the CLB Calculator to convert your IELTS, CELPIP, PTE, TEF or TCF scores to CLB or NCLC levels and check the program minimums. Higher language scores also earn more CRS points, so the minimum is a floor, not a target.

No education requirement, no job offer, no proof of funds

CEC has no education requirement and no job offer requirement. You do not need a degree or a job offer to qualify. Two things still matter:

  • Education still earns CRS points. To claim CRS points for foreign education, you need an Educational Credential Assessment. Without an ECA, your foreign education earns no CRS points, even though CEC itself does not require education.
  • Proof of funds is not needed under CEC. It is needed for the Federal Skilled Worker Program and the Federal Skilled Trades Program. See proof of funds for the full rules and the exemption letter the system still asks for.

Common mistakes

  • Counting student work. Co-op terms and part-time jobs during full-time study do not count, even if the work was skilled.
  • Counting self-employment. Freelance and self-employed work does not count, with an exception for some physicians.
  • Wrong TEER. Work in TEER 4 or 5 does not qualify. Check your occupation NOC code and TEER before you apply.
  • Expired language results. Results older than 2 years are not accepted. Retake the test before your profile expires.
  • Assuming CEC means no ranking. CEC gets you into the pool. Invitations still go to the highest CRS scores. A low score means a long wait, whatever program you qualify under.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much Canadian work experience do I need for CEC?

You need 1 year of skilled Canadian work experience in the last 3 years, equal to 1,560 hours, in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation. The work can be full-time, part-time, or a combination. Full-time means up to 30 hours a week for 12 months.

Does work while studying count toward CEC?

No. Work gained while you were a full-time student does not count, including co-op work terms. Self-employment also does not count, with an exception for some physicians.

What language level do I need for CEC?

It depends on your job TEER level. For TEER 0 or 1 jobs you need CLB 7 in all four abilities. For TEER 2 or 3 jobs you need CLB 5 in all four abilities. Language test results must be less than 2 years old.

Do I need proof of funds for CEC?

No. Proof of funds is not needed under the Canadian Experience Class. It is needed for the Federal Skilled Worker Program and the Federal Skilled Trades Program, unless an exemption applies.

Can I qualify for CEC if I worked in Quebec?

Work gained in Quebec counts toward the work experience itself, but you must plan to live outside Quebec to qualify for CEC. Quebec selects its own skilled workers.
