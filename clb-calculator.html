    How CLB conversion works

    IRCC does not use your raw test scores directly. It converts each ability to a CLB level (for English) or NCLC level (for French) using equivalency charts, and then awards CRS points from those levels. Four rules matter:

    • Each ability converts separately. Listening, reading, writing and speaking each get their own level. A high listening score does not lift a low writing score.
    • There is no averaging. IRCC does not average your four scores, and there is no overall-band conversion. Your lowest ability is often the one that decides which program minimums you meet.
    • A score between two rows counts as the lower row. The tables below show the lowest score that reaches each level. If your score falls between two rows, you get the lower level.
    • Results must be less than two years old when you complete your Express Entry profile and when you apply for permanent residence.

    The accepted tests are CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training, PTE Core, TEF Canada and TCF Canada. IELTS Academic, IELTS One Skill Retake, CELPIP-General LS and PTE Academic are not accepted for Express Entry. For TEF Canada, always use the Equivalence ancien score column on your results, never the score out of 699.

    What your CLB level means for CRS points

    Your first official language is worth up to 136 points without a spouse and 128 with a spouse, at up to 34 or 32 points per ability. The points per ability are:

    CLB / NCLC levelPoints per ability (with a spouse)Points per ability (without a spouse)

    CLB 71617

    CLB 92931

    CLB 9 in all four abilities is the most valuable threshold in the whole CRS. Beyond the first-language points, it unlocks the higher skill transferability combinations, worth up to 50 points each. A single ability below CLB 9 keeps you on the lower combination rows (as long as all four abilities are CLB 7 or higher; below CLB 7 in any ability, the language combinations score zero), which is why retaking a test to lift one weak ability can be worth far more than the points for that ability alone.     Frequently Asked Questions

    What is CLB?

    CLB stands for Canadian Language Benchmarks, the scale IRCC uses to describe English ability. NCLC (Niveaux de compétence linguistique canadiens) is the equivalent scale for French. For Express Entry, CLB and NCLC levels run from 1 to 12, and CLB 10 is the top level that earns CRS points.

    Can I average my four test scores into one CLB level?

    No. Each ability (listening, reading, writing, speaking) converts separately using the IRCC equivalency charts. There is no averaging and no overall-band conversion. Your lowest ability often decides which program minimums you meet and which CRS combinations you unlock.

    Is IELTS Academic accepted for Express Entry?

    No. Only IELTS General Training is accepted. IELTS Academic and IELTS One Skill Retake are not accepted for Express Entry. Similarly, CELPIP-General LS and PTE Academic are not accepted; the accepted tests are CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training, PTE Core, TEF Canada and TCF Canada.

    Which score do I use from my TEF Canada results?

    Use the Equivalence ancien score column on your TEF Canada results, never the Score out of 699 column. Using the wrong column gives the wrong NCLC level.

    How long are language test results valid?

    Language test results must be less than two years old when you complete your Express Entry profile and when you apply for permanent residence. Plan a retake before your results expire if you are still in the pool.

    What CLB level do I need for Express Entry?

    It depends on the program. The Federal Skilled Worker Program needs CLB or NCLC 7 in all four abilities. The Canadian Experience Class needs CLB 7 for TEER 0 and 1 jobs and CLB 5 for TEER 2 and 3 jobs. The Federal Skilled Trades Program needs CLB 5 in speaking and listening and CLB 4 in reading and writing.
