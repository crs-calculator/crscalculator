What Changed in the CRS on November 3, 2026

Last updated: November 17, 2026. Sources: IRCC CRS criteria page and Express Entry changes page, read November 2026.

On November 3, 2026, IRCC changed the Comprehensive Ranking System grid for the first time in years. The maximum score rose from 1,200 to 1,270, a new Labour Market Integration factor now rewards Canadian work experience and job offers in high-wage occupations, and job offer points returned after being removed in March 2025, this time without needing an LMIA. Calculators that still show a maximum of 1,200 are using the old grid.

This guide explains exactly what changed, what stayed the same, and what to do about it. To check your score under the new grid, use the CRS Calculator.

The Labour Market Integration factor

The Labour Market Integration factor is the centre of the change. It sits inside the core section (not on top of it) and is worth up to 150 points without a spouse and 140 with a spouse. It works as a choice: IRCC compares two routes and awards whichever gives more points, never both.

Route 1: Canadian work experience

Work-length points, plus a high-wage bonus if you have at least 12 months (1,560 hours) of qualifying Canadian work in high-wage occupations:

The tiers are checked highest first. Work can be continuous or not, full-time or equal part-time, and can be combined across more than one eligible high-wage job. Work in simultaneous jobs counts only up to full-time, 30 hours per week. Use the NOC lookup to check your occupation's tier: the list has 186 codes, 31 at the 2x tier, 89 at the 1.5x tier, and 66 at the 1.3x tier.

Example: a single applicant with 3 years of Canadian work (64 points) plus 12 months in a 1.5x occupation (40 bonus) has a work route total of 104. A job offer at the 2x tier would be worth 100. The work route wins, so the factor is worth 104. If the same person had only 1 year of work (40 points) and no bonus, the 2x job offer at 100 would win instead.

No LMIA needed, but genuineness is assessed

Since November 3, 2026, you do not need a Labour Market Impact Assessment for a job offer to qualify for CRS points, and having one gives no extra points. The offer must still be valid and genuine. An officer assesses genuineness, including whether the employer is active in the business and can fulfil the offer, whether the offer meets the employer's needs, and whether you are qualified and likely to carry out the job. A job offer can qualify for a program without earning CRS points: the two tests are separate.

What did not change

  • Skill transferability (maximum 100) is unchanged, including the combination rule and the 50-point cap per combination. Canadian work experience in this section still uses length only, not high-wage points.
  • Additional points (maximum 600) are unchanged: sibling 15, French bonus 25 or 50, Canadian education 15 or 30, provincial nomination 600.
  • Age, education and language points in the core section are unchanged.
  • Program eligibility (FSW, CEC, FST minimums) is unchanged, including the FSW 67-point grid.

Rescoring and the draw pause

IRCC rescored existing profiles under the new grid, and invitation rounds were paused for up to two weeks from November 3, 2026 while rescoring took place. A new or updated profile is scored under the rules in place when it is created or changed. Because every profile was rescored, cut-off scores from before the change are not a reliable guide to the new grid. See how cut-off scores work before comparing your new score with old draws.

What to do now

  1. Recheck your score with a calculator updated for the new grid, such as the CRS Calculator on this site. If your last check used a 1,200 maximum, it is out of date.
  2. Check your NOC tier in the NOC lookup. If your occupation is on the high-wage list and you have 12 or more months of Canadian work in it, you may have gained 20 to 70 points.
  3. Update your profile if your work experience, job offer, or language results have changed. Your score follows the information in your profile.
  4. Do not rely on old cut-offs. Wait for draws under the new grid before judging whether your score is competitive.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the new maximum CRS score?

1,270 points, up from 1,200. The core section maximum rose from 500 to 570 without a spouse and from 460 to 530 with a spouse, because of the new Labour Market Integration factor.

Do I need an LMIA for job offer points now?

No. Since November 3, 2026, you do not need a Labour Market Impact Assessment for a job offer to qualify for CRS points, and having one gives no extra points. The offer must be valid and genuine, in an eligible high-wage occupation, and an officer assesses genuineness.

Can I get points for both Canadian work and a job offer?

No. The Labour Market Integration factor awards whichever route gives more points, never both. Compare your work route total (work-length points plus any high-wage bonus) with your job offer tier points, and the higher number applies.

Did IRCC rescore existing Express Entry profiles?

Yes. IRCC rescored existing profiles under the new grid, and invitation rounds were paused for up to two weeks from November 3, 2026 while rescoring took place. Cut-offs from before the change are not a reliable guide to the new grid.

What did not change on November 3, 2026?

Skill transferability rules and the additional points section did not change. Age, education and language points in the core section also did not change. Only the Labour Market Integration factor, the core maximum, and job offer points changed.
