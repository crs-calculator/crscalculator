CRS Cut-Off Scores and Express Entry Draws, Explained

Last updated: November 17, 2026. Draw procedures: IRCC ministerial instructions for Express Entry rounds.

Your CRS score only matters in comparison with other candidates. In each invitation round, IRCC invites a set number of the highest-ranking candidates, and the score of the lowest-ranked candidate invited becomes that round's cut-off. This guide explains how rounds work, why cut-offs move, and why the numbers from before November 3, 2026 no longer apply. To check your own score under the current grid, use the CRS Calculator.

The lowest CRS score invited in each round is published in the draw results on the IRCC website, together with the tie-breaking rule for that round.

What a cut-off score is

Each round of invitations is governed by ministerial instructions that set the date, the round type, who is eligible, and how many invitations will be issued. IRCC ranks all eligible profiles by CRS score, invites from the top down until the invitations run out, and the lowest score invited is the cut-off for that round. Everyone invited scored at or above the cut-off, subject to the round's eligibility rules and the tie-breaking rule below.

A cut-off is a result, not a target IRCC sets in advance. It emerges from the number of invitations, the scores in the pool, and the round type. That is why cut-offs move from round to round, and why a past cut-off is never a promise about the next round.

Round types

Not all rounds invite the same people, so not all cut-offs are comparable:

  • General rounds invite the highest-ranking candidates across programs, with no program or category restriction.
  • Program-specific rounds invite only candidates eligible for one program, such as the Canadian Experience Class or the Provincial Nominee Program.
  • Category-based rounds invite candidates who meet a category requirement, such as French-language proficiency, healthcare occupations, or trades occupations. Category rounds have their own cut-offs, which are often very different from general rounds.

Provincial Nominee Program rounds always show much higher cut-offs than other rounds, because every candidate in them already holds a nomination worth 600 points. French-language rounds often show lower cut-offs, because the eligible pool is smaller. Comparing your score with the wrong round type will mislead you: compare only with recent rounds of the same type you would be invited under.

The tie-breaking rule

When more candidates share the cut-off score than there are invitations left, IRCC applies a tie-breaking rule based on the date and time the Express Entry profile was submitted. The ministerial instructions for each round publish a tie-break date and time: candidates at exactly the cut-off score are invited only if they entered the pool before that moment. Candidates above the cut-off are not affected by the tie-break. This is one practical reason to enter the pool as soon as you are eligible, rather than waiting: an earlier profile date wins ties.

Why cut-offs vary

  • Number of invitations. Larger rounds reach further down the ranking, so the cut-off falls. Smaller rounds stop higher up.
  • Pool composition. New high-scoring profiles entering the pool push cut-offs up; profiles expiring or being invited push them down.
  • Round type and category. As above, each round type draws from a different slice of the pool.
  • Grid changes. When IRCC changes the scoring rules, every score shifts, and old cut-offs stop being comparable. That is exactly what happened on November 3, 2026.

Why old cut-offs mislead after November 3, 2026

On November 3, 2026, IRCC raised the maximum score from 1,200 to 1,270, added the Labour Market Integration factor, and restored job offer points for high-wage occupations. Every existing profile was rescored under the new grid, and invitation rounds were paused for up to two weeks while rescoring took place. A score of, say, 480 under the old grid and 480 under the new grid are different achievements, because the points available and the way they are earned have changed.

Practical advice: do not judge your new score against any cut-off published before November 3, 2026. Wait for rounds held under the new grid, and compare only with rounds of the same type. If your score was checked with a calculator showing a 1,200 maximum, recheck it under the new grid first.

How to check the latest results

IRCC publishes every round's ministerial instructions, including the date, round type, number of invitations, lowest score invited, and tie-break date and time, on the official Express Entry rounds of invitations page. Check that page directly rather than relying on summaries, and note the round type before comparing any cut-off with your score.

What to do with your score

  1. Confirm your score is current. Use a calculator updated for the November 3, 2026 grid, and make sure your profile information is up to date.
  2. Find your round type. Work out which rounds you would be eligible for: general, program-specific, or a category such as French-language or an occupation category.
  3. Compare with recent same-type rounds only. One round is not a trend; look at several recent rounds of the same type.
  4. Improve what you can. If your score sits below recent cut-offs for your round type, the levers in how to increase your CRS score show what to work on, starting with language scores.

After receiving an invitation, you usually have 60 days to submit a complete application. The exact deadline is stated in your invitation and in the round's ministerial instructions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a CRS cut-off score?

The cut-off score of an invitation round is the CRS score of the lowest-ranked candidate invited in that round. Everyone invited scored at or above the cut-off, subject to the round's eligibility rules and the tie-breaking rule.

What is the tie-breaking rule in Express Entry draws?

When more candidates share the cut-off score than there are invitations, IRCC ranks them by the date and time their Express Entry profile was submitted. Only candidates who entered the pool before the published tie-break date and time are invited at exactly the cut-off score.

Why are old cut-off scores unreliable after November 3, 2026?

IRCC changed the CRS grid on November 3, 2026, raising the maximum from 1,200 to 1,270 and adding the Labour Market Integration factor, then rescored every profile. Scores under the new grid are not comparable with cut-offs set under the old grid, and rounds were paused for up to two weeks during rescoring.

What is a good CRS score?

It depends on the round type. Cut-offs vary by round, category, and the number of invitations. Compare your score only with recent rounds of the same type, and remember that a past cut-off is not a promise about the next round.

How long do I have to apply after receiving an invitation?

Usually 60 days from receiving the invitation to apply. The exact deadline is stated in your invitation and in the ministerial instructions for the round, so check those documents rather than relying on a general rule.
