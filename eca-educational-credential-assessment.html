Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) for Express Entry

Last updated: November 18, 2026. Sources: IRCC help centre ECA answer and IRCC ECA operational bulletin, read November 2026.

An Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) verifies that a foreign degree, diploma or certificate is valid and equal to a Canadian one. If your education was completed outside Canada, you need an ECA for Express Entry: to qualify under the Federal Skilled Worker Program, and to claim CRS points for your foreign education. This guide explains who needs one, the five designated organizations, the three professional bodies that must be used instead for some occupations, and the five-year validity rule.

The assessment must be for immigration purposes, from an organization or professional body designated by IRCC. An assessment done for another purpose, such as employment or licensing, does not count.

What an ECA is and is not

An ECA assesses your credential. It confirms the credential is genuine and states what Canadian level it equals, for example a bachelor degree or a two-year diploma. It does not license you to practise a regulated profession in Canada, and it does not decide your immigration application. Licensing and immigration decisions are separate processes with their own requirements.

Who needs one and who does not

SituationECA needed?
Principal applicant under the Federal Skilled Worker Program with education from outside CanadaYes
Claiming CRS points for foreign education, under any programYes
Claiming CRS points for a spouse or partner foreign educationYes, for the spouse education
Canadian degree, diploma or certificateNo
Qualifying under CEC or FST with no foreign education points claimedNo, for eligibility

Note the last row: the Canadian Experience Class and the Federal Skilled Trades Program have no education requirement, so an ECA is not needed to qualify. But without an ECA, your foreign education earns zero CRS points, which can cost you the invitation.

The 5 designated organizations

Most people get their ECA from one of these five organizations designated by IRCC:

The 3 professional bodies

If your primary occupation is one of the following, you must use the designated professional body for that occupation instead of the five organizations above:

Primary occupationNOC codeDesignated professional body
Architects21200Canadian Architectural Certification Board
Doctors31100, 31101, 31102Medical Council of Canada
Pharmacists31120Pharmacy Examining Board of Canada

Using the wrong body wastes time and money. If your primary occupation is architect, doctor or pharmacist, an assessment from one of the five general organizations does not meet the requirement. Check the IRCC list before you pay.

The 5-year rule

An ECA report must be less than 5 years old when used. An expired ECA means refusal on the Federal Skilled Worker Program, and it earns no CRS points. If your report is close to five years old, get a new assessment before you create or update your profile. Check the issue date on your report, not the date you received it.

How the ECA result feeds CRS education points

Your ECA result decides which education level you can claim in the CRS grid. Education points are a large part of the core score, up to 150 points for a single applicant, and they also feed the skill transferability combinations with language and Canadian work experience. See what the CRS score is made of for the full breakdown, and use the CRS Calculator to see what your assessed education level is worth.

In most cases you only need an assessment for your highest credential. To claim two or more credentials, you need an assessment for each one, and at least one must be for three or more years of study.

Cost and processing time

This guide does not publish ECA fees or timelines, because they differ by organization and change over time. Check the website of the organization you choose for its current fee and processing time. Start early: an assessment involves document verification with your school, and it takes weeks to months. A missing or expired ECA is one of the most common reasons a profile is delayed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an ECA for Express Entry?

An Educational Credential Assessment verifies that a foreign degree, diploma or certificate is valid and equal to a Canadian one. It must be obtained for immigration purposes, from an organization or professional body designated by IRCC.

Do I need an ECA for a Canadian degree?

No. You do not need an assessment for a Canadian degree, diploma or certificate. An ECA is only for education completed outside Canada.

Which organization should I use for my ECA?

Most people use one of five designated organizations, including World Education Services and the University of Toronto Comparative Education Service. If your primary occupation is architect, doctor or pharmacist, you must use the designated professional body for that occupation instead.

How long is an ECA valid?

An ECA report must be less than 5 years old when used. An expired report means you need a new assessment before you apply.

How much does an ECA cost and how long does it take?

Fees and processing times differ by organization and change over time, so this guide does not publish figures. Check the website of the organization you choose for its current fee and timeline, and start early, because an assessment takes weeks to months.
