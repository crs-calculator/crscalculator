The Federal Skilled Worker 67-Point Grid, Explained

Last updated: November 17, 2026. Sources: IRCC Federal Skilled Worker Program page and Educational Credential Assessment page, read November 2026.

Before your CRS score matters, you must qualify for an Express Entry program. For the Federal Skilled Worker Program (FSW), that means passing a separate 100-point selection grid with 67 points or higher. Score 67 or more and you can enter the Express Entry pool; score less and you do not qualify through this program. This guide explains the six selection factors, with points tables for language, experience, age and adaptability, the ECA rules for education points, and how the grid differs from CRS ranking.

Your FSW points come from six selection factors: language, education, work experience, age, arranged employment and adaptability.

Do not confuse the two systems. The FSW grid (out of 100, pass mark 67) decides eligibility. The CRS score (out of 1,270) ranks you in the pool after you qualify. They use different points and different rules.

Minimum requirements first

The selection grid only applies if you meet all of the program's minimum requirements:

  • Skilled work experience: at least one year of continuous, paid, full-time (or equivalent part-time) skilled work in the last 10 years, in a Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities (TEER) 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation. Full-time means 30 hours per week, 1,560 hours in a year. Hours above 30 per week do not count.
  • Language ability: CLB or NCLC 7 in all four abilities, proven with an approved test.
  • Education: a Canadian credential, or a foreign credential with an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) for immigration purposes.
  • Proof of funds: enough money to settle in Canada, unless you are currently able to work legally in Canada and hold a valid job offer from a Canadian employer.
  • Plan to live outside Quebec. Quebec selects its own skilled workers.

If you meet all minimum requirements, IRCC scores the six selection factors below. If you do not meet the minimums, the grid does not apply.

2. Education (maximum 25 points)

Education points depend on your highest credential as shown on your Canadian credential or your ECA report. The exact points for each assessment result are listed in the IRCC table on the Educational Credential Assessment page. Key ECA rules:

  • You need an ECA for immigration purposes from a designated organization. Most people use one of five designated organizations (including World Education Services and the University of Toronto Comparative Education Service); architects, doctors and pharmacists must use a designated professional body for their occupation.
  • Your ECA must be less than five years old when you complete your profile and when you apply. An expired ECA does not meet the requirement and you will need a new assessment.
  • In most cases you only need an assessment for your highest credential. To claim two or more credentials, you need an assessment for each one, and at least one must be for three or more years of study.
  • You do not need an assessment for a Canadian degree, diploma or certificate.

5. Arranged employment in Canada (maximum 10 points)

You can earn points for a job offer of at least one year from a single Canadian employer, for continuous, paid, full-time work (at least 30 hours per week, not seasonal) in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation. You must hold the offer before you apply as a federal skilled worker. Since November 3, 2026, you do not need an LMIA for a job offer to qualify, having an LMIA gives no extra CRS points or priority, and offers may be for an existing or new position with an existing or new employer. An officer assesses whether the offer is genuine.

After 67: the CRS takes over

Scoring 67 or higher means you may qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker Program and can submit a profile to the Express Entry pool. From there, your CRS score decides whether you receive an invitation. A high FSW grid score does not guarantee a high CRS score: the two systems reward different things. Once you know you qualify, check your ranking with the CRS Calculator and read how cut-off scores work. If you score below 67, check whether you qualify through the Canadian Experience Class or the Federal Skilled Trades Program instead, which do not use this grid.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FSW 67-point grid?

The FSW grid is the selection test for the Federal Skilled Worker Program. IRCC scores you out of 100 across six factors: language, education, work experience, age, arranged employment, and adaptability. You need 67 points or higher to qualify for the program and enter the Express Entry pool.

Is the FSW grid the same as the CRS score?

No. The FSW grid decides eligibility for the Federal Skilled Worker Program. The CRS score, out of 1,270, ranks you in the pool after you qualify. They use different points, different factors, and different maximums.

What language level do I need for the FSW grid?

CLB or NCLC 7 in all four abilities is the minimum for the Federal Skilled Worker Program. Below CLB 7 in any ability, you are not eligible. Language is worth up to 28 selection factor points: up to 24 for your first official language and 4 for a second official language at CLB 5 or higher in all four abilities.

Do I need an ECA for the FSW grid?

If your education is from outside Canada, yes. You need an Educational Credential Assessment for immigration purposes, less than five years old, showing your credential equals a completed Canadian secondary or post-secondary credential. You do not need an assessment for a Canadian degree, diploma or certificate.

What happens if I score less than 67 on the FSW grid?

You do not qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker Program and cannot enter the pool through it. You may still qualify through the Canadian Experience Class or the Federal Skilled Trades Program, which have different requirements and do not use the 67-point grid.
