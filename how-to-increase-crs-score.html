How to Increase Your CRS Score: 9 Ways to Gain Points

Last updated: November 17, 2026. Point values: IRCC CRS criteria page, November 3, 2026 grid.

Every CRS point comes from a published rule, so improving a score is a matter of finding which rules you can still use. This guide lists the nine levers that move a score most, with real point values from the grid in effect since November 3, 2026, in rough order of impact and speed. Nothing here is a guarantee, and every change must be backed by real, verifiable documents. To test each lever against your own profile, use the CRS Calculator.

If your goal is to improve your CRS score, start with the levers that add the most points for the least time and cost, then work down the list.

1. Raise your language scores to CLB 9

Language is usually the fastest lever, and CLB 9 in all four abilities is the most valuable threshold in the whole CRS. For a single applicant, moving from CLB 7 to CLB 9 in all four abilities adds 56 first-language points (17 to 31 per ability). Beyond that, CLB 9 unlocks the top skill transferability combinations, worth up to 50 points each, while a single ability below CLB 9 keeps you on the lower rows (as long as all four abilities are CLB 7 or higher; below CLB 7 in any ability, the language combinations score zero). Retaking a test to lift one weak ability can therefore be worth far more than the points for that ability alone. Use the CLB Calculator to see exactly where each ability stands and which score you need next.

2. Add a second official language, especially French

A second official language earns up to 24 core points without a spouse (22 with a spouse): CLB 5 or 6 earns 1 point per ability, CLB 7 or 8 earns 3, and CLB 9 or more earns 6. French adds more: NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills earns 25 additional points, or 50 if you also have CLB 5 or higher in all four English skills. French-language draws also run as their own category with their own cut-offs, so French can open a second path to an invitation, not just extra points.

3. Gain Canadian work experience

Each year of skilled Canadian work adds work-length points in the Labour Market Integration factor: 40 points for 1 year, 53 for 2 years, 64 for 3 years, 72 for 4 years, and 80 for 5 or more years, for a candidate without a spouse. Canadian work also feeds the skill transferability combinations, where 2 or more years unlocks the top rows. Work must be in a skilled Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities (TEER) 0 to 3 occupation for a Canadian employer.

4. Work in a high-wage occupation for 12 or more months

Since November 3, 2026, 12 or more months (1,560 hours) of Canadian work in high-wage occupations adds a bonus on top of work-length points: 70 points at the 2x tier, 40 at the 1.5x tier, or 20 at the 1.3x tier, with tiers checked highest first. Work can be combined across more than one eligible job. Check your occupation in the NOC lookup: 186 NOC codes are on the list, and many candidates do not realise their occupation qualifies.

5. Get a valid job offer in a high-wage occupation

A valid job offer in a high-wage occupation earns 100 points at the 2x tier, 70 at the 1.5x tier, or 50 at the 1.3x tier, with no LMIA needed since November 3, 2026. The offer must be valid and genuine, and an officer assesses genuineness. Remember the trade-off: the Labour Market Integration factor awards the work route or the job offer route, whichever is higher, never both. A job offer helps most when your Canadian work experience is short. See the November 2026 changes guide for the full rules.

6. Add education, including Canadian education

Education points rise with each level: a bachelor's degree earns 120 points without a spouse, two or more credentials earn 128, a master's or professional degree earns 135, and a PhD earns 150. A second credential can also lift the skill transferability education combinations to the top rows. A Canadian post-secondary credential adds 15 additional points for a one or two year credential, or 30 for three years or longer, on top of the core education points. Foreign credentials need an ECA for immigration purposes to earn points.

7. Use spouse factors

An accompanying spouse or partner who is not a citizen or permanent resident can add up to 40 points: up to 10 for their education, up to 20 for their first official language (CLB 9 or more earns 5 per ability), and up to 10 for their Canadian work experience. A spouse language test is often the cheapest of these three. Note that scoring with a spouse uses slightly lower core columns than scoring without one, so always check your exact situation in the calculator rather than assuming.

8. Get a certificate of qualification (trades)

If you hold a certificate of qualification from a Canadian province or territory for a trade occupation, the certificate combination in skill transferability is worth 25 points with CLB 5 or higher in all four abilities, or 50 points with CLB 7 or higher. For trades candidates this is one of the largest single gains available.

9. Consider a provincial nomination

A provincial or territorial nomination adds 600 points, which is almost always enough for an invitation. But nomination streams are competitive, each province has its own requirements and occupation priorities, and a nomination usually means committing to settle in that province. Treat it as a parallel track: keep improving your CRS score while you explore nomination streams that fit your occupation and profile.

Smaller gains that still count

A brother or sister living in Canada who is 18 or older and a citizen or permanent resident adds 15 points. And do not overlook timing: age points fall each year after 29, so entering the pool sooner rather than later protects the age points you have today, and keeping your language results current (less than two years old) avoids losing points to expiry.

What does not help, and what to avoid

  • An LMIA no longer adds points. Since November 3, 2026, having an LMIA gives no extra CRS points. Do not pay for one expecting points.
  • A job offer outside a high-wage occupation earns zero CRS points, even if it is genuine and helps with program eligibility. The two tests are separate.
  • Never misrepresent anything. False work history, fake job offers, or altered documents can lead to your application being refused and a period of inadmissibility from Canada. Every point you claim must be backed by documents IRCC can verify.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fastest way to increase a CRS score?

Raising language scores to CLB 9 in all four abilities is usually the fastest lever. For a single applicant, moving from CLB 7 to CLB 9 in all four abilities adds 56 first-language points, and it can also unlock higher skill transferability combinations worth up to 50 points each.

Does a job offer still help my CRS score?

Yes, since November 3, 2026. A valid job offer in a high-wage occupation earns 50, 70 or 100 points by wage tier, with no LMIA needed. It replaces, not adds to, your Canadian work experience points: the Labour Market Integration factor awards whichever route is higher.

How many points does French add to a CRS score?

NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills earns 25 additional points, or 50 if you also have CLB 5 or higher in all four English skills. French can also earn second-language core points of up to 24, and French-language draws have their own cut-offs.

Does an LMIA add CRS points?

No. Since November 3, 2026, an LMIA is not needed for a job offer to qualify for CRS points, and having one gives no extra points. What matters is whether the offer is valid and genuine and in an eligible high-wage occupation.

Can my spouse's profile raise my CRS score?

Yes. An accompanying spouse can add up to 40 points through their education (up to 10), language (up to 20) and Canadian work experience (up to 10). Note that scoring with a spouse uses slightly lower core columns, so check both options in the calculator to see which applies to your situation.
