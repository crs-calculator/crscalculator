How the CRS Works

The Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) ranks candidates in the Express Entry pool. IRCC invites the highest-ranking candidates to apply for permanent residence in regular draws. Your score comes from four parts:

Your CRS score for Express Entry is the total of these four parts, and this CRS points calculator adds them up as you answer.

SectionWhat it coversMaximum
Core factorsAge, education, first and second official language, and Labour Market Integration (Canadian work experience or a high-wage job offer)570 without a spouse, 530 with a spouse

The Labour Market Integration factor

Since November 3, 2026, the new Labour Market Integration factor sits inside the core section and is worth up to 150 points without a spouse and 140 with a spouse. It works as a choice: IRCC awards points for Canadian work experience (work-length points plus a high-wage bonus of 20, 40 or 70 for 12 or more months in a high-wage occupation) or for a valid job offer in a high-wage occupation (50, 70 or 100 points by wage tier), whichever is higher. You never get both. No LMIA is needed for job offer points, and having one gives no extra points.

High-Wage Occupation Lookup (NOC)

Check whether your occupation is on the IRCC high-wage list, and which tier applies. The list has 186 National Occupational Classification (NOC) codes in three tiers, based on 2023 to 2025 wage data, in effect since November 3, 2026. Type a job title or a 5-digit NOC code.

See the full high-wage NOC list with all 186 occupations, tier filters, and the bonus and job offer points for each tier.

    Tiers: 2x means the occupation median wage is at least twice the average median wage of the Canadian workforce; 1.5x means at least 1.5 times; 1.3x means at least 1.3 times. The highest tier you qualify for applies. The list can change, and if it changes after you receive an invitation, the tier that applied on your invitation date is used.

    How to Improve Your CRS Score

    These are the levers that move a CRS score most, in order of typical impact. Nothing here is a guarantee, and every change must be backed by real, verifiable documents.

    Read the full guide: how to increase your CRS score, with point values for all nine levers.

    • Raise your language scores, especially to CLB 9. CLB 9 in all four abilities unlocks the higher skill transferability combinations (up to 50 points each) and the full first-language points. Even one ability below CLB 9 keeps you on the lower combination rows (as long as all four abilities are CLB 7 or higher; below CLB 7 in any ability, the language combinations score zero).
    • Gain Canadian work experience. Each year adds work-length points, and 12 or more months in a high-wage occupation adds a bonus of 20, 40 or 70 points.
    • Get a valid job offer in a high-wage occupation. Worth 50, 70 or 100 points depending on the wage tier, with no LMIA needed. Remember it replaces, not adds to, your work experience points.
    • Add French. NCLC 7 or higher in all four French skills earns 25 points, or 50 if you also have CLB 5 or higher in English. French-language draws also have their own, often lower, cut-offs.
    • Add education. A second credential of three or more years, a master's, or a Canadian credential can add core points, skill transferability points, and additional points.
    • Consider a provincial nomination. A nomination adds 600 points, which is almost always enough for an invitation, but nomination streams are competitive and have their own requirements.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the maximum CRS score?

    The maximum CRS score is 1,270 points: up to 570 core points without a spouse (530 with a spouse), up to 40 spouse points, up to 100 skill transferability points, and up to 600 additional points. Without a provincial nomination, the highest possible score is 670.

    What is the Labour Market Integration factor?

    The Labour Market Integration factor is new in the CRS since November 3, 2026. It awards points for either Canadian work experience, including extra points for work in high-wage occupations, or a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation, whichever gives the higher score. It is worth up to 150 points without a spouse and 140 points with a spouse, and it sits inside the core section, not on top of it.

    Can I get points for both Canadian work experience and a job offer?

    No. IRCC compares the two and awards whichever gives more points, never both. The work route combines work-length points with a high-wage bonus, while the job offer route awards 50, 70 or 100 points by wage tier.

    What counts as a high-wage occupation?

    A high-wage occupation is a job whose NOC median wage across Canada is at least 1.3 times the average median wage of the Canadian workforce. IRCC groups 186 NOC codes into three tiers: at least twice the average median wage, at least 1.5 times, and at least 1.3 times. The list is based on 2023 to 2025 wage data and took effect on November 3, 2026.

    Do I still need an LMIA for job offer points?

    No. Since November 3, 2026, you do not need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) for a job offer to qualify for CRS points, and having one gives no extra points. The offer must still be valid and genuine, and it must be in an eligible high-wage occupation to earn points.

    How do language test scores convert to CLB levels?

    Each ability (listening, reading, writing, speaking) converts separately using IRCC equivalency charts. The accepted tests are CELPIP-General, IELTS General Training, PTE Core, TEF Canada and TCF Canada. For TEF Canada, use the Equivalence ancien score column on your results, not the score out of 699. Results must be less than two years old. Use the CLB calculator to convert your scores and check program minimums.

    What is a good CRS score?

    It depends on the draw. Cut-off scores vary by round and category, and IRCC paused invitation rounds for up to two weeks from November 3, 2026 while profiles were rescored, so older cut-offs are not a reliable guide. Check the latest ministerial instructions and draw results on the IRCC website before relying on any cut-off. See how cut-off scores and draws work.

    Is this CRS score calculator Canada-specific?

    No. This CRS score calculator follows the IRCC grid, which applies to every Express Entry candidate, whether you live in Canada or abroad. Where you live does not change your score, but Canadian work experience and Canadian education do.
