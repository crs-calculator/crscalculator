High-Wage NOC Lookup: All 186 Occupations, By Tier

Last updated: November 17, 2026. List: IRCC high-wage occupations page, in effect November 3, 2026, based on 2023 to 2025 wage data.

Since November 3, 2026, working in a high-wage occupation (or holding a job offer in one) earns extra CRS points through the Labour Market Integration factor. Use this NOC code lookup to check whether your occupation is on the IRCC high-wage NOC list and which of the three wage tiers applies. Type a job title or a 5-digit NOC code.

How your tier affects your CRS score

Your tier feeds the Labour Market Integration factor, worth up to 150 points without a spouse and 140 with a spouse. It works as a choice between two routes, and IRCC awards whichever gives more points, never both:

  • Work route. Work-length points (up to 80 without a spouse, 70 with a spouse) plus a high-wage bonus of 70, 40 or 20 points if you have at least 12 months, or 1,560 hours, of qualifying Canadian work in high-wage occupations. The tiers are checked highest first: 12 or more months at the 2x tier earns 70, otherwise 12 or more months combined at the 1.5x tier earns 40, otherwise 12 or more months combined at the 1.3x tier earns 20. Work can be combined across more than one eligible job, and simultaneous jobs count only up to full-time, 30 hours per week.
  • Job offer route. A valid job offer in a high-wage occupation earns 100 points at the 2x tier, 70 at the 1.5x tier, or 50 at the 1.3x tier. No LMIA is needed since November 3, 2026, but the offer must be valid and genuine, and an officer assesses genuineness.

High-wage status is based on the NOC code's median wage across Canada, not on your personal salary. The tiers were set from Job Bank, Labour Force Survey and Census data over the last three years, using the highest level met in that period. The list can change, and if it changes after you receive an invitation, the tier that applied on your invitation date is used. For the full rules, see the November 2026 CRS changes guide, and to see your score with your tier applied, use the CRS Calculator.

If your occupation is not on the list

If your NOC code does not appear here, it is not on the high-wage list, so it earns no high-wage bonus and no job offer points. Your Canadian work experience still earns work-length points, and your other CRS factors are unaffected. Before concluding, double-check that you have the right NOC code: the code must match your actual job duties, not just your job title. The Government of Canada NOC search can help you confirm the code that fits your duties.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes an occupation high-wage?

An occupation is high-wage when the median wage for its NOC code across Canada is at least 1.3 times the average median wage of the Canadian workforce. IRCC groups 186 NOC codes into three tiers: at least twice the average median wage, at least 1.5 times, and at least 1.3 times. The list is based on 2023 to 2025 wage data and took effect on November 3, 2026.

Does my own salary decide whether my job is high-wage?

No. High-wage status is based on the NOC code's median wage across Canada, not on your personal salary. Two people in the same NOC code have the same tier, whatever they are paid individually.

How long must I work in a high-wage occupation to earn bonus points?

At least 12 months, or 1,560 hours, of qualifying Canadian work in high-wage occupations. The work can be continuous or not, full-time or equal part-time, and can be combined across more than one eligible high-wage job. Work in simultaneous jobs counts only up to full-time, 30 hours per week.

Can the high-wage list change?

Yes. IRCC can update the list. If the list changes after you receive an invitation to apply, the tier that applied on your invitation date is used.

Do I need an LMIA for job offer points in a high-wage occupation?

No. Since November 3, 2026, you do not need a Labour Market Impact Assessment for a job offer to qualify for CRS points, and having one gives no extra points. The offer must still be valid and genuine, and it must be in an eligible high-wage occupation to earn points.
