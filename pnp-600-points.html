Provincial Nominee Program: The 600 CRS Points, Explained

Last updated: November 18, 2026. Sources: IRCC Provincial Nominee Program Express Entry pages and CRS criteria page, read November 2026.

A nomination through the Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) adds 600 additional CRS points, the single largest points boost in Express Entry. Without a nomination, the highest possible CRS score is 670. With one, the ceiling reaches 1,270. This guide explains how the 600 points for a provincial nomination work, the step-by-step nomination process, the 30-day accept window, and what the 600 points do and do not guarantee.

One point matters first: a nomination comes from a province or territory, not from IRCC. Provinces and territories select candidates for their own labour needs through their own streams, then confirm the nomination to IRCC electronically.

What the Provincial Nominee Program is

The Provincial Nominee Program lets provinces and territories nominate people who want to live in that province or territory and who can contribute to its economy. There are two routes:

RouteHow it worksCRS points
Express Entry routeYou also qualify for a federal Express Entry program and hold a pool profile. The province confirms the nomination electronically, and you accept it in your profile.600 additional points
Non-Express Entry routePaper-based process outside Express Entry, with different steps and timelines.None, it is not part of CRS ranking

This guide covers the Express Entry route, which is the one that affects your CRS score.

How to get a nomination

There are two ways to get a nomination under a province or territory PNP program:

  • Apply to the province or territory directly. Decide in which province or territory you want to live and work, check its PNP eligibility requirements on its own website, and apply under one of its streams if you meet them.
  • Show your interest in your Express Entry profile. Provinces and territories can search the pool to find candidates to nominate. Update your profile under Nomination and selection to show the province where you want to live and work, or all provinces and territories.

If a province is interested in your profile, it sends a notification of interest message to your account. That message is not a nomination and does not guarantee you will be nominated. You still apply to the province stream and meet its requirements. You can receive more than one notification of interest at a time, but you can only hold one nomination at a time.

The 600 points, step by step

  1. Enter the pool. Qualify for at least one of the three Express Entry programs (Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class, or Federal Skilled Trades) and create your profile.
  2. Get nominated. A province or territory decides to nominate you under one of its streams.
  3. Have the nomination confirmed. The province must confirm your nomination electronically. After you submit your profile, contact the province directly, outside your account, and give them your Express Entry profile number and job seeker validation code.
  4. Accept or reject within 30 calendar days. Once the province confirms, you get a message in your account asking you to accept or reject the nomination. You have 30 calendar days to choose accept or not accept.
  5. 600 points are added. If you accept, the system finds you meet the PNP requirements, sends a confirmation letter to your account, and awards 600 additional points.
  6. Wait for an invitation round. With the added points, you rank near the top of the pool. Invitations still come by round, including PNP-specific rounds.

The 600-point cap and a worked example

Six hundred points is the maximum additional points you can get. That cap shapes everything about the nomination:

  • If you already have other additional points, such as the sibling points, the French bonus, or Canadian education points, only the 600 points for your nomination are added. The other additional points do not stack on top.
  • If you already have 600 additional points, a nomination adds no more points. You can only get the 600 additional points once.
  • Without a nomination, the ceiling is 670 (570 core plus 100 skill transferability for a single applicant). With a nomination, it reaches 1,270.

Worked example. A single applicant with 420 CRS points and no additional points yet accepts a provincial nomination. The score becomes 420 plus 600, which is 1,020. Check the same arithmetic on the CRS Calculator by selecting a provincial nomination in the additional factors section.

What the 600 points do and do not guarantee

A nomination is not an invitation, and an invitation is not permanent residence. The 600 points move you to the top of the ranking, and PNP-specific rounds invite nominated candidates, but IRCC still invites by round, and you must still meet program requirements and admissibility checks after an invitation.

What the 600 points do: they put you above almost every non-nominated candidate in the pool, they make a PNP round invitation very likely, and they end the wait that most candidates face at lower scores. What they do not do: they do not skip the invitation round, they do not waive medical, criminality or document checks, and they do not guarantee the province stream requirements stay the same. If you reject or ignore a nomination, you are not eligible for the PNP unless another province or territory nominates you, although you stay in the pool under any other program you qualify for. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a PNP nomination guarantee an invitation to apply?

No. Invitations are issued by round, and nothing is automatic. A nomination adds 600 points, which puts you near the top of the pool and is almost always enough in a PNP-specific round, but you still wait for a round that invites you.

Can I get 600 PNP points twice?

No. You can only get the 600 additional points once. If you already have 600 additional points, a nomination adds no more points. You can only hold one nomination at a time.

What happens if I reject a provincial nomination?

You will not be eligible for the PNP unless another province or territory nominates you. You stay in the Express Entry pool as long as you stay eligible for an Express Entry program, and you may be invited under another program you are eligible for.

Must I live in the province that nominates me?

You apply to the province or territory where you want to live and work, and the nomination is issued by that province for its own program. The IRCC process starts with deciding which province or territory you want to live and work in, so choose the province you genuinely plan to settle in.

Do I need to qualify for a federal program for the PNP Express Entry route?

Yes. For the Express Entry route, you must qualify for at least one of the three Express Entry programs (Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class, or Federal Skilled Trades) to enter the pool. The non-Express Entry route is a separate, paper-based process.
