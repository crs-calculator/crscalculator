Proof of Funds for Express Entry: Amounts, Rules, Documents

Last updated: November 18, 2026. Source: IRCC proof of funds page, read November 18, 2026. Amounts table updated July 7, 2025 by IRCC.

Proof of funds is how you show IRCC that you have enough money to settle in Canada. If you are invited to apply, you must give written proof that you have this money. This guide covers who needs proof of funds, who is exempt, the amounts by family size, how family size is counted, and the bank letter IRCC accepts.

The amounts below are the minimums from the table IRCC updated on July 7, 2025, verified on the live IRCC page on November 18, 2026. IRCC updates these numbers every year, based on 50% of the low income cut-off totals, so check the official proof of funds page for the current table before you apply.

Canadian Experience ClassNo

Exempt applicants still upload a document. The system asks all applicants for a proof of funds document. If you do not need to provide proof, you must upload a letter explaining either that you were invited under the Canadian Experience Class, or that you hold a valid job offer and authorization to work in Canada. Skipping the upload causes delays.

Keep your funds up to date in your profile. The system may find you eligible for more than one program, and you do not always know ahead of time which program you will be invited under.

How family size is counted

The amount you need depends on the size of your family. To calculate family size, include:

  • Yourself
  • Your spouse or common-law partner
  • Your dependent children
  • Your spouse's or common-law partner's dependent children

You must include your spouse or common-law partner and dependent children, even if they are Canadian citizens or permanent residents, and even if they are not coming to Canada with you. Forgetting a non-accompanying family member is a common mistake that leaves you short of the required amount.

What documents IRCC accepts

For proof, you must get official letters from any banks or financial institutions where you have an account. The letters must be printed on the financial institution letterhead and include:

  • The bank or institution contact information: address, telephone number and email address
  • Your name
  • Your outstanding debts, such as credit card debts and loans
  • For each current banking and investment account: account numbers, the date each account was opened, current account balances, and the average balance for the past 6 months

Your funds must be available to you both when you apply and when a permanent resident visa is issued, if your application is approved. A letter showing the money on one day is not enough if the money is gone later.

Money that does and does not count

  • Borrowed money does not count. You cannot borrow this money from another person.
  • Property equity does not count. You cannot use equity on real property as proof of settlement funds.
  • Joint accounts count. If your spouse is coming with you, you can count money you hold together in a joint account.
  • A spouse-only account can count. You may be able to count money in an account under your spouse's name only, but you must prove you have access to the money.

You must be able to use this money to pay for your family living costs, even if your family members are not coming with you.

Common mistakes

  • Counting borrowed money or property equity. Neither counts, and IRCC checks the source of funds.
  • Wrong family size. Non-accompanying spouses, partners and children still count toward the amount.
  • Missing the 6-month average balance. The bank letter must include the average balance for the past 6 months, not just the current balance.
  • Letting the balance drop. Funds must be available when you apply and when the visa is issued. A large withdrawal between those dates creates a problem.
  • Skipping the exemption letter. Exempt applicants who upload nothing face delays, because the system asks everyone for the document.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who needs proof of funds for Express Entry?

Proof of funds is needed for the Federal Skilled Worker Program and the Federal Skilled Trades Program. It is not needed under the Canadian Experience Class, or if you are authorized to work in Canada and hold a valid job offer, even under FSW or FST.

How much money do I need for proof of funds?

The amount depends on family size. Under the table updated July 7, 2025, one person needs $15,263 and a family of four needs $28,362. The full table is on this page. IRCC updates the amounts every year, so check the official page for the current table.

Can I borrow the money for proof of funds?

No. You cannot borrow the money from another person, and equity on real property does not count. The funds must be available to you both when you apply and when a permanent resident visa is issued.

Does money in a joint account count for proof of funds?

Yes, if your spouse is coming with you, you can count money you hold together in a joint account. Money in an account under your spouse's name only can also count, but you must prove you have access to it.

Do I still need a document if I am exempt from proof of funds?

Yes. The system asks all applicants for a proof of funds document. If you do not need to provide proof, you must upload a letter explaining that you were invited under the Canadian Experience Class, or that you hold a valid job offer and authorization to work in Canada.
