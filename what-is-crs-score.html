What Is the CRS Score? A Complete Guide to Express Entry Ranking

Last updated: November 17, 2026. Grid: IRCC CRS criteria page, read November 2026, updated for the November 3, 2026 changes.

The Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) is the points system Canada uses to rank candidates in the Express Entry pool. When you submit an Express Entry profile, IRCC gives you a score out of 1,270 points. In each invitation round, IRCC invites the highest-ranking candidates to apply for permanent residence. Your score does not decide whether you are eligible for Express Entry, it decides where you stand in line.

In short, your CRS score for Express Entry is the number that decides whether you receive an invitation to apply. This guide explains how that number is built, section by section, with the full points tables and a worked example.

This guide explains the four sections of the score, the full points tables, and a worked example, using the grid in effect since November 3, 2026. To check your own score, use the CRS Calculator.

The four sections

SectionWhat it coversMaximum
A. Core factorsAge, education, first and second official language, and Labour Market Integration (Canadian work experience or a high-wage job offer)570 without a spouse, 530 with a spouse
B. Spouse factorsSpouse's education, language and Canadian work experience40
C. Skill transferabilityCombinations of education, language, Canadian work, foreign work and trade certificates100
D. Additional pointsSibling in Canada, French language bonus, Canadian education, provincial nomination600

Spouse factors apply only when your spouse or common-law partner is coming with you and is not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident. If your spouse is not coming with you, or is a citizen or permanent resident, you are scored with the without-a-spouse columns, which give slightly more core points. Without a provincial nomination, the highest possible score is 670.

A second official language earns up to 24 points without a spouse and 22 with a spouse: CLB 5 or 6 earns 1 point per ability, CLB 7 or 8 earns 3, and CLB 9 or more earns 6.

Labour Market Integration factor

New since November 3, 2026, this factor sits inside the core section and is worth up to 150 points without a spouse and 140 with a spouse. IRCC awards points for Canadian work experience (work-length points plus a high-wage bonus of 20, 40 or 70 for 12 or more months in a high-wage occupation) or for a valid job offer in a high-wage occupation (50, 70 or 100 points by wage tier), whichever is higher, never both. No LMIA is needed for job offer points. See the November 2026 changes guide for the full rules and the NOC lookup to check your occupation's tier.

B. Spouse factors (maximum 40)

When your spouse or partner accompanies you and is not a citizen or permanent resident, their education earns up to 10 points, their first official language up to 20 points (CLB 5 to 6: 1 per ability, CLB 7 to 8: 3, CLB 9 or more: 5), and their Canadian work experience up to 10 points (1 year: 5, 2 years: 7, 3 years: 8, 4 years: 9, 5 or more years: 10).

C. Skill transferability (maximum 100)

Skill transferability rewards combinations. Within each combination, IRCC takes the higher of the two variants, each combination is capped at 50, and the section total is capped at 100:

CLB 9 in all four abilities is the key threshold: it unlocks the top row of the language combinations. Canadian work experience in this section uses length only, not high-wage points.

The section is capped at 600, so a nomination alone reaches the cap. A nomination almost always leads to an invitation, but nomination streams are competitive and have their own requirements.

The same profile with CLB 7 instead of CLB 9 in all four abilities scores 426, a difference of 56 points from language alone. This is why language scores are usually the fastest lever for improving a score. See how to increase your CRS score.

CRS score vs. eligibility

Your CRS score ranks you, but it does not make you eligible. Eligibility comes from one of the three Express Entry programs (Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class, or Federal Skilled Trades), each with its own minimum requirements. The Federal Skilled Worker Program adds a separate 100-point selection grid with a 67-point pass mark, explained in the FSW 67-point grid guide. Once you are eligible and in the pool, only your CRS score and the draw's cut-off decide whether you receive an invitation. See how cut-off scores work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the CRS score?

The CRS (Comprehensive Ranking System) score ranks candidates in the Express Entry pool. IRCC invites the highest-ranking candidates to apply for permanent residence in regular draws. The score is out of 1,270 points and comes from four sections: core factors, spouse factors, skill transferability, and additional points.

What is the maximum CRS score?

The maximum CRS score is 1,270 points: up to 570 core points without a spouse (530 with a spouse), up to 40 spouse points, up to 100 skill transferability points, and up to 600 additional points. Without a provincial nomination, the highest possible score is 670.

Is the CRS score the same as the FSW 67-point grid?

No. The FSW 67-point grid is an eligibility test for the Federal Skilled Worker Program: you need 67 out of 100 to enter the pool. The CRS score ranks you once you are in the pool. They use different points and different rules.

How long does a CRS score last?

An Express Entry profile is valid for 12 months. Your score can change during that time if your circumstances change, for example a birthday, new work experience, or new test results, or if IRCC changes the grid. Update your profile whenever your information changes.

Can my CRS score go down?

Yes. Age points fall as you get older, language test results expire after two years, and IRCC grid changes can rescore your profile. The November 3, 2026 grid change rescored all existing profiles.
